# Reflection

When I first thought about this project, I assumed the main issue with the hostel register was that the guard wasn't doing his job properly — like he was being lazy about collecting information. But after actually going to the gate and watching what happens during the evening rush, I realized the problem is completely different. Students are the ones who fill the register, not the guard. They rush through it because they're in a hurry to leave, and the guard can't really force them to slow down and fill every single field. So the missing data isn't a discipline problem — it's a design problem. The register has no way to tell someone "hey, you skipped a field" the way a form on a phone would.

If I did this project again, I would cut the search and filter feature. It sounds useful but honestly, the guard doesn't need to search through the data — he just needs to know which entries are incomplete so he can catch students when they come back and ask them to fill the gaps. The bar chart breakdown was more useful than I expected though, because it showed clearly that return time is the biggest problem, which makes sense because students just walk past the counter when they come back at night.

AI helped me a lot with writing the Streamlit code. I described what I wanted — metrics, a table, download buttons — and it generated most of the app structure. It also helped me read the handwritten register from the photo, which saved me maybe an hour of squinting at messy handwriting. The data extraction was not perfect but it got me maybe 80% of the way there, and I fixed the rest by looking at the photo myself.

Where AI wasted my time was with the photo blurring. It kept getting the face positions wrong because it couldn't tell exactly where the people were in the image. I went through like 4-5 rounds of "no, the face is lower... no, more to the right" before it got it roughly right. I could have done that in MS Paint in 5 minutes honestly.

From talking to the guard, I learned that the warden checks the register randomly, not on a fixed schedule. The guard told me he gets scolded if entries are incomplete, even though it's the students who fill the register, not him. That changed how I thought about the tool — it's not just about catching missing data, it's about giving the guard a way to protect himself by catching problems early, before the warden shows up.

This tool should not be trusted to decide if a student is safe. If a student goes out and doesn't come back and the return time is blank, that could mean they just forgot to sign in, or it could mean something is wrong. The tool can't tell the difference. It also shouldn't replace the physical register — the paper register is what the administration considers the official record. And it definitely can't verify if the information students write is actually true. Someone could write "Canteen" as their destination and go somewhere else entirely. The tool only checks completeness, not truthfulness.
